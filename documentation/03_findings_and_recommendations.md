# Findings and Recommendations

## How to read this
Each finding follows: **finding → evidence → business implication → recommendation → measure**. Findings are associations, not proof of cause.

## KPI definitions

| KPI | Definition |
|---|---|
| Conversion rate | Sessions with an order ÷ all sessions |
| Funnel step rate | Sessions reaching a step ÷ sessions reaching the previous step |
| Net revenue | Gross revenue − refunds (at item level) |
| Refund rate (items) | Refunded items ÷ items sold |
| Average order value | Revenue ÷ orders |

## Findings

**F1. The biggest loss in the funnel is between product detail and cart.**
Of 472,871 sessions, 261,231 reach a product list page and 210,214 view a product. Only 94,953 reach the cart, so 115,261 sessions drop out at that step, the largest loss in absolute terms. Overall conversion is 6.83%.
*Implication:* the product page is the highest-value place to test changes.
*Recommendation:* test clearer delivery, price and product detail on the product page. *Owner:* UX lead.
*Measure:* percentage of product detail sessions that reach the cart, weekly.

**F2. Desktop converts about 2.75 times as well as mobile.**
Desktop converts at 8.50% (27,805 orders from 327,027 sessions); mobile at 3.09% (4,508 from 145,844). Mobile is 31% of sessions.
*Implication:* a third of traffic arrives on a device that converts poorly.
*Recommendation:* review the mobile checkout journey before spending on acquisition. *Owner:* UX lead with the engineering team.
*Measure:* mobile conversion rate, monthly.

**F3. Refund rates differ sharply by product.**
The Birthday Sugar Panda loses 6.04% of items to refunds and The Original Mr. Fuzzy 5.11%, against 2.23% for The Forever Love Bear and 1.28% for The Hudson River Mini bear. The two highest-volume products carry the most refunds.
*Implication:* refunds reduce net revenue most on the best-selling products.
*Recommendation:* review product descriptions and quality complaints for the top two products before the next promotion. *Owner:* merchandising lead.
*Measure:* item refund rate by product, monthly.

**F4. Channel quality varies widely, and channel size is not channel quality.**
gsearch nonbrand earns the most net revenue ($1.07M). The socialbook pilot converts at 1.08% on 5,095 sessions, against 6.66% for gsearch nonbrand. Sessions with no UTM tag convert at 7.34%.
*Implication:* budget decisions need both volume and conversion.
*Recommendation:* review socialbook pilot spend against its conversion before the next budget cycle. *Owner:* marketing manager.
*Measure:* net revenue per session, by channel.

**F5. Multi-item orders carry a much higher order value.**
23.87% of orders contain more than one item. They average $89.25 against $50.82 for single-item orders.
*Implication:* add-ons or bundles may lift order value, but multi-item buyers may differ in other ways.
*Recommendation:* test an add-on offer at the cart with a control group. *Owner:* merchandising lead.
*Measure:* average order value per session, test against control.

**F6. Repeat sessions convert slightly better, but order values are similar.**
Repeat sessions make up 16.61% of sessions, convert at 7.83% against 6.64% for new sessions, and have similar average order values ($60.54 against $59.86).
*Implication:* a retention programme would have a small effect on conversion and little on order value in this data.
*Recommendation:* treat retention spend as a hypothesis to test, not a priority. *Owner:* marketing manager.
*Measure:* repeat session share and conversion, monthly.

## Caveats
- Partial months (March 2012 and March 2015) are not used to claim trends.
- Acquisition cost is not in the data, so return on ad spend is not measured.
- The store is a fictional sample, so the numbers illustrate the method.
